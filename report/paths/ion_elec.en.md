**The idea.** Take the laser out of the entangling gate: currents in the trap chip's electrodes make a microwave field whose gradient couples a hyperfine ion qubit to the shared motion (`g_elec`); drive traces are shared and local tuning electrodes select the ions [D][I42]. A microwave photon reaches no excited state, so laser gates' scattering floor vanishes: 8.4×10⁻⁵ on two ions, without ground-state cooling [D][I4]. Speed is the price, since a stronger gradient puts the ion nearer heating surfaces: 225.8 µs per gate [D][I4] against ~70 µs on Helios [D][I1]. The bet: control that scales like electronics, ~200 sources for 1,000 ions [S] (§7.5).

**What it needs.** The gate *requires* chip-integrated microwave control (`ct_ionmw`), which *requires* current traces in the trap: the chip is the control system, with no fallback to laser gates. Lasers remain for cooling, state preparation and fluorescence readout. Carrier, gate, control, readout and interconnect have one technology each; elsewhere the options *replace* each other: chain bus or shuttling, qLDPC or high-rate codes (both *require* all-to-all), Relay-BP or matching, trap microfabrication or a 300 mm CMOS line. Without transport the derived syndrome round is gate-limited: 1.52 ms, 1.4 ms of it in gates [S] (§7.4).

**Where it stands.** Six machines, two devices, no code, decoder or link run. QUARTET (Oxford Ionics/IonQ), at the UK's National Quantum Computing Centre, is the largest at 32 qubits (Table 8.3), a count no primary source gives; its evidence paper shows a seven-zone chip for up to 10 qubits [D][I42]. JION (eleQtron with Forschungszentrum Jülich), inaugurated on 3 Sep 2026, runs 30 ytterbium ions with no published fidelity [P][I43]. Hence no best error in Table 8.3: the 99.99% is a two-ion record [D][I4]. Superion 256 is orderable: SkyWater-made chips, ions trapped in prototypes, no 256-qubit fidelity [C][I30]; IonQ Superion 10K (commercial 2028) [R][I30] and NFQC-1k (≥1,000 physical, 50 logical qubits by 2030) [R][I34] are plans; QUDORA NFQC is an emulator.

**Exceptions and borrowings.** No cell leaves the architecture's slots; the exceptions are in the evidence. Superion 256 alone fills all ten layers, from plans: a qLDPC code from a resource estimate [S][I29], a claimed photonic link [C][I30], a Relay-BP cell reading "algorithm not named" while IonQ's published decoder runs on one CPU [S][I13] (mislabelled), a metastable-encoding alternate citing nothing. Layers 7–9 are IonQ's intentions. IonQ Superion 10K and NFQC-1k leave four layers undisclosed: early stage. JION's MAGIC (magnetic-gradient-induced coupling) addresses ions by frequency in a static gradient, a second mechanism inside a technology defined by the near-field one: drawn too widely. QUARTET's shuttling alternate has no timeline. Universal Quantum's iQPU (QCCD architecture) borrows the gate and control technologies and fits both architectures equally: a hybrid.

**The next test.** Superion 256 is due at NVIDIA's research centre and Florida International University in 2027 [C][I31][I32]. Confirm if it is commissioned by end-2027 near 10⁻⁴ two-qubit error over the full register, publishing gate time, heating and crosstalk (§5.2). Demote if it slips beyond 2027, or if its error lands tenfold above the two-ion record once hundreds of microwave channels share a die: electronic control would then remain the single-qubit layer of laser-entangled machines.
