**The idea.** The qubit is a microwave resonator mode, the Bosonic cavity mode (`cavity`), whose dominant error is photon loss. In the Cat-code encoding (biased noise) two-photon dissipation holds two coherent states apart: bit-flips fall exponentially with mean photon number n̄, phase-flips rise linearly, so a line of d cats, not a d × d patch, corrects the rest; lattice density is the price. The bet: photons buy the distance a lattice buys with qubits, if the bias survives the entangling gate. The alternate GKP grid encoding shares the hardware, not the bet: it corrects small displacements and passes up an analogue syndrome.

**What it needs.** The encoding *requires* two-photon dissipation on a mode, and Bosonic concatenation a biased or GKP inner qubit: the architecture rests on the phase-flip rate during a two-qubit gate. Every measured entangling gate is ancilla-mediated (`g_bos`), through a transmon that does not preserve the bias; the Bias-preserving cat–cat CNOT that would *replace* it is an empty slot with one simulated proposal (Jul 2026) [S][B4]. Six layers offer one technology each. Lithographed modes *replace* 3D machined cavities, and the 3D branch *conflicts* with the lattice: centimetre-scale bodies cannot tile a dense 2D array (open [B5]).

**Where it stands.** {{N_A_CAT_MACH_CAP}}, {{N_A_CAT_DEV}} (Table 8.3): Alice & Bob Boson 4, Helium 2 ("Galvanic Cat"), Helium (on-premise system) and the planned Graphene, Lithium (48 cats) and Beryllium (250) [R][B21][B24]; AWS Ocelot; Nord Quantique single-mode GKP and Tesseract. The largest, Helium (18 cats), deployed June 2026 without logical data [C][B10]. Bit-flip times reach 430 s (Boson 4) and a preliminary 44-min mean (Helium 2) [C][B1]; only Ocelot ran an outer code: repetition at 1.75% (d=3, n̄=1) and 1.65% (d=5, n̄=1.5) per 2.8 µs cycle, distance and photon number moving together, with CX phase-flip 9.6(4)×10⁻² against bit-flip 3.5(4)×10⁻³ [D][B3]. Helium 2's 5.8×10⁻² is a single-qubit Z gate, not a two-qubit error, and Ocelot's 9.6×10⁻² is a phase-flip rate per error-correction cycle, not a gate error, so Table 8.3 shows no two-qubit error for the architecture [C][B1]. Nord Quantique single-mode GKP: 8.1×10⁻³ logical error per round, SPAM below 10⁻³ at survival 0.24 × 0.39 [D][B5].

**Exceptions and borrowings.** No off-slot cell, no better-fitting architecture; the shared absences, real-time decoder and interconnect, mark an early stage. The Nord Quantique pair: its decoder cell is an Atlas gap, `∅G-auton` (stabilisation without classical feed-forward), so layer 8 is drawn too narrowly; its code cell reads Bosonic concatenation though neither runs an outer code, like Boson 4, a single cat with zero logical qubits on its roadmap [C][B14]. GKP joins by hardware, not by the bias bet: for its idea the architecture is drawn too widely. Borrowers: Aurora (continuous-variable photonics) takes the GKP and code technologies; the dual-rail architecture's Aqumen Seeker, SUSTech biased-erasure cavity qubit and D-Wave gate-model dual-rail system take cavity and gates.

**The next test.** Two expectations to March 2028 (§5.2). First, Helium's roadmap rung, one logical qubit at 10⁻² [R][B14]: phase-flip ≤ 10⁻² per cycle at n̄ ≈ 10 confirms; Helium still without logical data at end-2027 demotes. Second, a measured two-cat gate holding bit-flip time within 10× of idle; if 2028 opens with ancilla-mediated gates only, the 126,133- and 758-cat estimates, which assume 10⁻³ phase-flip per cycle, lose their premise [S][B17][B18]. GKP owes a published GKP–GKP gate.

**What the builders say.** AWS chose cat qubits because they suppress bit flips in the hardware itself, as its Ocelot chip showed [D][B20], and estimates that the approach cuts the cost of error correction by up to 90% [C][B20]. Alice & Bob plans Graphene, 100 logical qubits for early industrial quantum advantage, by 2030 [R][B21]; it reported bit-flip times above one hour, four times what that machine needs, before peer review [C][B1], and built its on-premise Helium system to encode a first logical qubit in as few as 18 cat qubits [C][B10]. Nord Quantique builds the correction into each qubit, one cavity per logical qubit [C][B22], and expects its first machines with more than 100 logical qubits by 2029 [R][B23].
