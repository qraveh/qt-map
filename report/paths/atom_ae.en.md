**The idea.** Ytterbium and strontium have a metastable ³P₀ level one clock-line photon above the ground state. Store the qubit there (the Metastable ('omg') erasure encoding on the Alkaline-earth atom (Yb/Sr) — erasure-native), and ground-state imaging light, dark to the qubit, reveals atoms that decayed out of it: errors arrive located, as erasures. The bet: a worse gate for a cheaper code. At 98% conversion the surface-code threshold rises from 0.94% to 4.15% [S][X8]; measured, a metastable CZ errs 0.016(1), 38(6)% flagged, against 0.0060 raw on the ground-state ¹⁷¹Yb qubit [D][N8][N2].

**What it needs.** A relay: the encoding *requires* the metastable manifold; the Mid-circuit erasure check *requires* the encoding, since only a qubit dark to imaging can be checked unread; Erasure-adapted codes *require* the flags and Correlated / loss-aware decoding, since a plain matcher cannot use them. Break a link and the machine is an alkali array with a heavier atom. Five layers have one option; *replaces* edges name the retreats: ground-state or optical encodings, surface code, millisecond imaging, one-shot loading. One *conflict*: reload light (MOT, repumper) depletes stored atoms; a repumper-free MOT and ³P₀ shelving, 13 s lifetime, mitigate it [P][N37].

**Where it stands.** Five machines, three devices (Table 8.3). Phoenix (Gen-1) and Gen-2, 1,180 ¹⁷¹Yb atoms (Oct 2023), is the largest gate-capable device, with the only two-qubit figure, 4.0×10⁻³ [C][N13]; its toric code ran 90 rounds, but suppression vanished with reloading included [D][N13]. Magne, 1,225 physical for ~50 logical, is being installed for early 2027 [R][N22][N29][N30]. The metastable Yb-171 erasure array (Princeton, 20 atoms) slowed [[4,2,2]] decay 1.9(4)× with unconditional erasure decoding [D][N8]. MAQCS and DiNAQC: a 1,000-qubit demonstrator at TRL 5 [C][N33]; the neutral-ytterbium QPU began hardware validation in August 2026 [C][N38]. 28 of 59 cells documented.

**Exceptions and borrowings.** All five fit here best; only Princeton runs the bet's encoding, check, code and decoder as primaries. Phoenix and Magne encode in the ¹⁷¹Yb ground-state nuclear spin (Yaqumo by inference); their erasures are imaged atom losses, as on rubidium [D][N5]. Phoenix's metastable-encoding cell is denied by its own note (mislabelled), its code is an Atlas gap, `∅G-toric` (code technology too narrow), its row joins ⁸⁷Sr and ¹⁷¹Yb generations. MAQCS encodes on the Sr clock line, 10–100 ms coherence [P][N26], in an optical lattice (`∅G-lattice`, shared with QUIONE), no code, an undisclosed core link; planqc's error-corrected line, LOGIQC, is ytterbium [C][N34]. Yaqumo's fast readout is inferred from 17.6 µs imaging of spinless ¹⁷⁴Yb [P][N39]: early stage. No machine has the Atom–photon cavity interface. Borrowers: OQC GENESIS (check), Superion 256 (encoding), IBEX Q1 (optical qubit), QUIONE (carrier). The architecture is drawn by species; erasure-native rests on one laboratory.

**The next test.** Consistent with §5.2: by mid-2027 Magne crosses the 1,180-qubit frontier (§8.5, F1b) and delivers 50 logical qubits, decoded with erasure flags on unconditioned data; loss detection alone confirms the carrier, not the encoding. By end-2027 a group outside Princeton and Caltech converts over 70% of a metastable CZ's error with residual below 3×10⁻³; if none passes 50% and the next Yb memory again loses suppression under reloading, the architecture demotes to a species choice within the alkali bet.
