**The idea.** A point defect in diamond (NV, SiV, SnV) or silicon (T centre) (`defect`) has a spin-selective optical transition: its electron spin emits a photon entangled with it, and nearby nuclear spins under hyperfine-conditional microwave gates (`g_mwspin`) keep a small memory. Heralded photons (`ic_spinphoton`), not on-chip wiring, join these few-qubit nodes. Table 8.3's bet fuses two on one carrier: a room-temperature NV accelerator and cryogenic network nodes. Both forgo a dense lattice; for the nodes, link rate, not gate error, is the clock.

**What it needs.** The own technologies *require* the carrier — gates its spin register, fluorescence readout its optical transition, the link its spin–photon interface — and the carrier *requires* a host crystal, whose only technology is diamond (`fab_diamond`). Five technologies are borrowed: encoding, readout, baseband control, nearest-neighbour lattice, qLDPC code. Two clash: the code *conflicts* with the lattice, needing degree-6 checks that here only the optical link supplies. No decoder (§7.6): a code named, none decoded.

**Exceptions and borrowings.** All three fit here best; none fits whole. Photonic connects any-to-any through the optical link (`∅G-optlink`), which its SHYPS qLDPC code needs, claimed at 20× fewer physical qubits per logical [C][Q26]; its silicon host has no technology (`∅G-sicolour`): layers 4 and 10 are drawn too narrowly. Quoll lacks link, code and decoder, and its microwave-and-laser control has no technology (`∅G-nvroom`): a processor on a network architecture, drawn too widely. Fujitsu's prototype adds a photonic-IC foundry (`fab_pic`), states no connectivity and shares the control gap at 1.55 K: an early hybrid. Only Photonic uses the namesake link; the architecture's spin gates (`g_mwspin`) recur as the layer-3 alternate of the quantum-dot and donor architectures.

**The next test.** By the technology briefs (§5.2 has no defect row), confirmation by March 2028 is an unconditional inter-node gate above 90%, a heralded link above 1 kHz, or link rate and fidelity from Fujitsu's multi-module prototype. Demotion: Photonic's links still without rate and fidelity, and the SnV modules without a qubit count, at end-2027; then the network bet is repeater and sensing technology; Quoll needs its own architecture.

**What the builders say.** Fujitsu, with TU Delft and QuTech, built a working diamond-spin prototype with tin-vacancy centres on photonic chips [C][Q21], chose the route for its optical links between modules and plans a multi-module prototype by 2027 [R][Q21]. Photonic argues that T-centre spin qubits in silicon, linked by light, give high connectivity, efficient error correction and a fit with telecom networks [C][Q38]; in 2024 it ran a teleported CNOT between qubits in different cryostats [C][Q39]. Quantum Brilliance bets on nitrogen-vacancy qubits at room temperature and pressure, which its CEO says decides where the machines can go and who can use them [C][Q40]; Fraunhofer IAF bought its rack-mounted QB-QDK2.0 [C][Q41].
