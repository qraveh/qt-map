**The idea.** One excitation shared between two modes, cavity modes or transmons, is the Dual-rail (erasure) encoding (`enc_dualrail`): logical |10⟩ and |01⟩; loss on either rail lands in |00⟩, outside the code space. A Mid-circuit erasure check sees that exit without reading the logical state: the dominant error arrives located, cheaper to correct than an unknown Pauli error. The price: two modes per qubit and a check every cycle. The bet: fail more often, but visibly; with 98% of errors converted, the surface-code threshold rises from 0.94% to 4.15% [S][X8].

**What it needs.** The chain is hard: the encoding *requires* the check, and Erasure-adapted codes *require* its flags; without the check the qubit is merely lossier, without a flag-using decoder the heralds are wasted. The check's tax: 384 ns on transmons, residual 6.0(2)×10⁻⁴ against 2.54(1)×10⁻² erasure per check [D][B8], inside a gate-limited round of at least 2.8 µs [S] (§7.4). Six layers (2–4, 7–9) hold one technology each; the gate technology *requires* the modes plus a transmon ancilla. Alternates: vertical delivery and on-chip flux-DAC multiplexing *replace* room-temperature lines, lithography *replaces* 3D machining, and transmon rails escape the cavities' *conflict* with a dense lattice.

The Yale/Quantum Circuits cavity CZ: ~500 ns, 0.53% erasure per gate, post-selected Pauli error below 0.1%, about 80% of its error flagged [D][B7]. Seeker's 1.0×10⁻³ is post-selected on no heralded erasure (0.53% per gate), so Table 8.3 shows no unconditioned two-qubit error for the architecture [C][B9]. GENESIS publishes no entangling gate — its gate cell reads undisclosed, not none — so Table 8.3 does not count it gate-capable; the table's largest gate-capable device is the SUSTech four-dual-rail-qubit processor, 8 qubits, whose logical CNOT (98.1 % process fidelity at a 13 % erasure rate) is post-selected [D][B25]. No machine has run an erasure-decoded code.

**Exceptions and borrowings.** One off-slot cell and no better-fitting architecture: the SUSTech four-dual-rail-qubit processor entangles its transmon rails through tunable couplers (`g_tc`), a gate the architecture's slot does not hold — it expects ancilla-mediated bosonic gates [D][B25]. Two more sit loosely. GENESIS's carrier alternate is an Atlas gap, `∅G-dimon`: one fixed-frequency multimode transmon, three islands and two junctions, two transmon-like modes [C][S40], beyond the transmon technology (layer 1 too narrow); its gate is undisclosed (a data gap), its code cell none: error detection by post-selection. The SUSTech qubit is not dual-rail: it encodes in the vacuum and two-photon Fock states of one cavity, with erasure bias above 265 [D][S41]; its encoding cell is mislabelled, or the architecture too narrow, its idea being loss-to-erasure, not two rails. The D-Wave system is a hybrid: annealer flux-DAC control. Other architectures borrow the check (Phoenix (Gen-1) and Gen-2, Magne; alkaline-earth Rydberg) and the encoding (Lucy, Belenos; fusion-based photonic), as alternates.

**The next test.** Two expectations to March 2028 (§5.2). First, the D-Wave 17-qubit system publishes a logical error 2× below physical by mid-2027; if end-2027 passes with no dual-rail machine reporting logical error falling more than 2× per distance step on unconditioned data, the architecture is demoted. Second, the first dual-rail memory of at least ten rounds at distance 3, erasure flags in the decoder, survival reported; the CHIPS-backed design at 100 physical qubits per logical [R][B19] presumes it.

**What the builders say.** D-Wave designs its dual-rail qubits to flag about 90% of errors as they occur, so that correction needs fewer physical qubits [C][S45]; its entangling gate runs in about 500 ns, with about 0.5% erasure and under 0.1% residual error per gate [D][B7]. It plans 17 physical qubits in 2026 and 100 logical qubits running more than a million operations by 2032 [R][S45]. OQC says its dual-rail Dimon needs ten times fewer physical qubits per logical qubit than today's leading approaches [C][S46], installed its GENESIS machine in a New York City data centre in September 2025 [C][S47], and plans 200 logical qubits by 2028 and 50,000 by 2034 [R][S46].
