**The idea.** One excitation shared between two modes, cavity modes or transmons, is the Dual-rail (erasure) encoding (`enc_dualrail`): logical |10⟩ and |01⟩; loss on either rail lands in |00⟩, outside the code space. A Mid-circuit erasure check sees that exit without reading the logical state: the dominant error arrives located, cheaper to correct than an unknown Pauli error. The price: two modes per qubit and a check every cycle. The bet: fail more often, but visibly; with 98% of errors converted, the surface-code threshold rises from 0.94% to 4.15% [S][X8].

**What it needs.** The chain is hard: the encoding *requires* the check, and Erasure-adapted codes *require* its flags; without the check the qubit is merely lossier, without a flag-using decoder the heralds are wasted. The check's tax: 384 ns on transmons, residual 6.0(2)×10⁻⁴ against 2.54(1)×10⁻² erasure per check [D][B8], inside a gate-limited round of at least 2.8 µs [S] (§7.4). Six layers (2–4, 7–9) hold one station each; the gate station *requires* the modes plus a transmon ancilla. Alternates: vertical delivery and on-chip flux-DAC multiplexing *replace* room-temperature lines, lithography *replaces* 3D machining, and transmon rails escape the cavities' *conflict* with a dense lattice.

**Where it stands.** Five machines, three devices (Table 8.3): Aqumen Seeker, eight dual-rail cavity qubits (Quantum Circuits Inc., now D-Wave, 2024) [C][B9][B13]; OQC GENESIS, 16 sites, deployed September 2025; the SUSTech biased-erasure cavity qubit; the AWS dual-rail transmon erasure line, a component; the D-Wave gate-model dual-rail system, 17 qubits due in 2026 [R][B16]. The Yale/Quantum Circuits cavity CZ: ~500 ns, 0.53% erasure per gate, post-selected Pauli error below 0.1%, about 80% of its error flagged [D][B7]. Table 8.3's best two-qubit error, 1.0×10⁻³ on Seeker, excludes heralded erasures; with them it is ~10⁻² [C][B9]. GENESIS, the table's largest gate-capable device, publishes no entangling gate; its gate cell reads undisclosed, not none. No machine has run an erasure-decoded code.

**Exceptions and borrowings.** No off-slot cell and no better-fitting path, but two sit loosely. GENESIS's carrier alternate is a Map gap, `∅G-dimon`: one fixed-frequency multimode transmon, three islands and two junctions, two transmon-like modes [C][S40], beyond the transmon station (layer 1 too narrow); its gate is undisclosed (a data gap), its code cell none: error detection by post-selection. The SUSTech qubit is not dual-rail: it encodes in the vacuum and two-photon Fock states of one cavity, with erasure bias above 265 [D][S41]; its encoding cell is mislabelled, or the path too narrow, its idea being loss-to-erasure, not two rails. The D-Wave system is a hybrid: annealer flux-DAC control. Other paths borrow the check (Phoenix (Gen-1) and Gen-2, Magne; alkaline-earth Rydberg) and the encoding (Lucy, Belenos; fusion-based photonic), as alternates.

**The next test.** Two expectations to March 2028 (§5.2). First, the D-Wave 17-qubit system publishes a logical error 2× below physical by mid-2027; if end-2027 passes with no dual-rail machine reporting logical error falling more than 2× per distance step on unconditioned data, the path is demoted. Second, the first dual-rail memory of at least ten rounds at distance 3, erasure flags in the decoder, survival reported; the CHIPS-backed design at 100 physical qubits per logical [R][B19] presumes it.
