**The idea.** Keep the alkali tweezer array with its lasers, transport and imaging; drop the gate: global Rydberg lasers drive the whole register under one Ising Hamiltonian, Analog Rydberg Hamiltonian evolution (`g_rydanalog`) on the Ground–Rydberg analog qubit (`enc_gr`). Waveforms Ω(t), Δ(t) and atom geometry are the program, a bit-string snapshot the output. The bet: for many-body physics (G1), native Hamiltonian evolution reaches hundreds of atoms now. It gives up memory, gates and any route to fault tolerance.

**What it needs.** The evolution *requires* the ground–Rydberg basis, a Rydberg level in the carrier and global Rydberg lasers; imaging, an imaging transition. Code, decoder and interconnect are empty by design (§7.6): here analog simulation stops being a quantum computer in the register's sense. The encoding is a basis, not a memory: |r⟩ cannot be shelved; Aquila's T2* is 5.8 µs, programs stop at 4 µs [C][N40]; errors integrate coherently with no instant for a syndrome, so no code attaches and no decoder has input. Without an entangling gate they are devices, not processors (Table 8.3 prints no gate-capable size, no two-qubit error). The one *replaces* edge swaps the species.

**Exceptions and borrowings.** The dual-mode Pasqal machines mirror each other: Fresnel / Fresnel 2 runs analog as primary, its digital mode (Hyperfine / clock-state qubit, Rydberg-blockade CZ) and PIC-generated tweezers as alternates; Orion Alpha / Beta ("Ruby") / Gamma, on the alkali architecture, swaps the modes; all else matches. Each fits its own architecture seven technologies to five: the boundary with atom_rb is a mode switch in layers 2–3 of one hardware (hybrid). The gate separates simulator from processor; the line fault tolerance needs, a code, neither has crossed. QUIONE lacks encoding, gate and Rydberg state: a Bose–Hubbard gas in a lattice (`∅G-lattice`, shared with MAQCS) under a carrier named for erasure-native tweezer qubits, here by elimination (four technologies in slot): the architecture is too narrow for lattice quantum-gas simulators. Caltech's erasure-excising Sr Rydberg simulator [N9], which the new alkaline-earth edges describe, is not in the register.
