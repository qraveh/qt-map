**The idea.** One static chain in a linear Paul trap: its collective motional modes (`cx_bus`) couple every pair, so a Mølmer–Sørensen gate entangles any two ions without moving them, and each ion gets its own acousto-optically steered beam (`ct_ionaod`). The qubit is hyperfine (Yb⁺, Ba⁺) or the optical S–D qubit of ⁴⁰Ca⁺ (`enc_opt`: AQT, Innsbruck, PIAST-Q). It gives up length: N ions share N modes in a fixed bandwidth, so gates slow as the chain grows, to a median 672 µs on Forte's 30 ions [D][I27]. The bet: connectivity is free inside a chain of tens of ions; scale is bought at its edge with photonic links between chains, the modular architecture of Monroe et al. [S][I37].

**What it needs.** The gate *requires* spin–motion coupling and free-space laser fields, which *require* bulk optics. The two defining stations *conflict* (open): mode crowding makes the gate slower and less faithful with each ion; the mitigations (zoned short chains, electronic gates on ≤4-ion segments) belong to other ion architectures. qLDPC and high-rate codes *require* the chain's all-to-all: four logical qubits in 18 ions [D][I11]. Encoding offers three mutually *replacing* stations (hyperfine, optical, metastable); carrier, gate, connectivity, readout and interconnect have one station each; the interconnect, Ion–photon photonic link, carries the scaling bet at a best 250 s⁻¹ [D][I16] against ~10⁴ s⁻¹ needed [S] (§2.3). Layer 10 is drawn too narrowly: planar surface-electrode chips only, while IBEX Q1 runs in a machined blade trap [D][I39] and the 512-ion crystal in a monolithic 3-D trap [D][I17].

**Where it stands.** Thirteen machines, ten devices: IonQ's Aria, Forte and 40-ion Ba-133 qLDPC testbed; AQT's IBEX Q1; PIAST-Q; Sandia's QSCOUT; Qudoor's AbaQ; the Innsbruck and Maryland/Duke traps; Tsinghua's analog 512-ion simulator [D][I17]. Tempo is deployed but not counted: its 100 qubits and "99.9% fidelity" are targets without gate times [C][I27]. Quantum Art and the Rosatom 50-qubit machine are announcements. The largest gate-capable device, the 40-ion testbed, reached memory break-even within error bars, 3.95(68) s logical against 3.84(48) s physical, with leakage post-selection [D][I11]. Maryland/Duke's 3.0×10⁻³ is a logical Clifford error after correction, not a physical figure, and is no longer counted [D][I38]; Table 8.3's best error is therefore the vendor's 4.0×10⁻³ on Aria and Forte [C][I3]. IonQ, the largest vendor, has put its successor, Superion 256, on electronic gates [C][I30].

**Exceptions and borrowings.** Maryland/Duke carries three: that logical figure; a Bacon–Shor subsystem code with no station (`∅G-subsystem`; its high-rate alternate encodes one qubit); a link cell merging a separate experiment, the 250 s⁻¹ record [D][I16], with Oxford's 9.7 s⁻¹ link [D][I15]: a lineage, not a machine. Innsbruck borrows ion shuttling (segmented traps) and the magic-state station [D][I40]: a lineage spanning both laser architectures, and a code layer drawn too narrowly; its measurement-free feedback is a gap (`∅G-coherent`). IBEX Q1's shuttling alternate describes AQT's research traps, not the product: mislabelled. Forte's CMOS-foundry alternate projects IonQ's SkyWater route, first used for the 256-qubit chips of 2026 [C][I30], onto a 2022 machine. The testbed and Tempo decode by offline beam search (`∅G-beam`), outside layer 8; Tempo's code cell repeats the testbed's run. Tsinghua's crystal is analog under global beams (`∅G-isinganalog`, `∅G-crystal`): the architecture is drawn too widely, and ions lack an analog architecture. Quantum Art's link is filed as undisclosed, yet its paper segments one crystal into cells with reconfigurable optical potentials [S][I41]: neither bus nor transport, a gap. Qudoor's control and all Rosatom cells: data gaps. Borrowers: the electronic-control architecture takes the motional bus (JION, QUARTET, Superion 256); planqc's MAQCS the optical qubit.

**The next test.** The link decides: confirm if an ion–photon link exceeds 10³ s⁻¹ by end-2027; demote if none passes ~10× the 250 s⁻¹ record by end-2028. At chain scale, confirm if per-pair errors and gate times appear for Tempo or any chain above 40 ions by end-2027; if disclosure stays at headline level while IonQ ships electronic gates, the architecture narrows to racks and laboratories, leaving early fault tolerance to other ion architectures.
