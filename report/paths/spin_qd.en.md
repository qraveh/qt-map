**The idea.** A Gate-defined quantum-dot spin (`qd_spin`) is a transistor-sized gate stack: printed by a 300 mm CMOS foundry (`fab_cmos`), entangled by a pulse on the barrier to its neighbour, the Exchange gate (`g_exch`), read by Spin-to-charge conversion (`ro_s2c`), and coherent 1.31 ms (Hahn echo) on foundry ²⁸Si [D][Q7]. The bet: dots uniform enough to share calibration, with control moved into the cryostat, scale like transistors. The price: a readout-limited clock, 8.5 µs derived [S] against ~100–300 µs as run [G] (§7.4), and reach only to neighbours unless spins move.

**What it needs.** Exchange *requires* the dots and baseband pulses: its strength depends exponentially on barrier voltage, and HRL puts ~80% of its CNOT error on control and calibration [D][Q2]. Carrier, cryo-CMOS, conveyor and the Cryogenic signal fan-out all *require* the foundry; the fan-out also *requires* cryo-CMOS behind it: Intel's Pando Tree serves up to 64 qubit terminals at 10–20 mK from one signal and nine digital lines of Horse Ridge II, 0.156 lines per terminal [C][Q30]. The surface code *requires* a 2D nearest-neighbour lattice, which no register device shows; qLDPC *conflicts* with it (long-range checks), needing the conveyor that *replaces* it. Crossbar lines *conflict* with per-pair calibration. Five layers hold one technology; none links modules.

**Where it stands.** {{N_A_SPIN_QD_MACH_CAP}}, {{N_A_SPIN_QD_DEV}} (Table 8.3): demonstrated, the HRL exchange-only silicon spin processor (18 EO qubits from 54 quantum dots — Table 8.3's largest gate-capable device, level at 18 with Groove's, added to the register on 26 Sep 2026 [D][Q2]), Tunnel Falls (Intel; 12 qubits [D][Q9]), the foundry 8-qubit array (Diraq/imec, exchange in one of four pairs [D][Q7]) and Groove Quantum's 18-qubit germanium spin processor (April 2026, no fidelities or paper [P][Q44]); deployed, RacQ (Equal1; sold as Bell-1 until May 2026, the name ESA still uses [C][Q23][Q46]), installed at ESA's Frascati centre in July 2026 [C][Q46], and QM-One (Quantum Motion, count unpublished [Q10]); announced, the Diraq 8-qubit silicon spin quantum computer at Equinix Sydney and the Hitachi x Intel 18A spin programme; planned, Alloy Pioneer (Quobly), cloud access by end-2026 [R][Q16]. The best two-qubit error among devices, 3.0×10⁻³, is HRL's [D][Q2]; Equal1's product page claims 99.3% (7.0×10⁻³) [C][Q31] where a press survey prints 98.4% [P][Q32]; no other machine publishes one. Off-register bests: 99.04–99.56% on foundry two-qubit devices [D][Q1]. HRL's bit-flip repetition code, error falling 4.7× from distance 3 to 5, sequenced from 4 K, is the architecture's only error-correction run [D][Q2].

**Exceptions and borrowings.** No better-fitting architecture; two technology cells sit off the slots, HRL's error-detection code (`code_detect`) and RacQ's cryogenic decoder (`dec_cryo`), an alternate not yet run. Until 26 Sep 2026 the primaries described an absent machine — all eight rows used Single-spin (Loss–DiVincenzo) encoding, none Exchange-only encoding; HRL's device, now IBM's [Q18], is now the ninth row and the architecture's only carrier of its primary encoding, with its repetition code and parity decoder as Atlas gaps (`∅G-repetition`, `∅G-parity`). The foundry 8-qubit array holds the only in-slot code cell, a roadmap qLDPC with shuttling as alternate (early stage), and carries 1.1×10⁻² from other devices at 1 K [D][Q5]. The on-die control of RacQ and QM-One is an Atlas gap (`∅G-qsoc`). Tunnel Falls mixes cryo-CMOS and room-temperature drive (hybrid); Equinix control is undisclosed. Only Tunnel Falls documents its fan-out; the cells of QM-One, RacQ and the Hitachi programme are inferred. Four drive single spins by ESR or EDSR, the Microwave / optical spin gates (`g_mwspin`) that layer 3 lists as the alternate; their gate cells name only the exchange gate. Borrowers: cryo-CMOS on five superconducting machines; the foundry on thirteen, eight ion traps.

**The next test.** By March 2028 (§5.2): a 300 mm array above 20 qubits with all-pairs two-qubit fidelity ≥ 99.5% and µs-class readout confirms; Diraq's 2029 target (150 k physical, 1 k logical [R][Q14]) cut again demotes. If the Equinix system (due October 2026 [C][Q20]) and Alloy Pioneer reach 2028 without their own two-qubit error, the best number stays a vendor's.

**What the builders say.** Diraq argues from the foundry: its qubits are made like the chips in phones and laptops, a path to millions of qubits on one chip [C][Q33]. Quobly, making its chips on STMicroelectronics' 300 mm line, targets one million qubits by 2032, after cloud access to its first machine, Alloy Pioneer, by end-2026 [R][Q16]. Hitachi, with Intel and AIST, plans error-corrected prototypes of 100 qubits in fiscal 2028 and 1,000 in fiscal 2030 on Intel's 18A process [R][Q34]. HRL answers the wiring problem with a cryogenic CMOS controller that ran error correction on 18-qubit devices without room-temperature control [D][Q2]. Equal1 fits a silicon-spin computer cooled to 0.3 K into one 19-inch rack [C][Q23]; Quantum Motion's QM-One takes three [C][Q10].
